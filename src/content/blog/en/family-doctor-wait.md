Ask around Ward 2 and you will hear the same worry again and again: it is hard to find a family doctor, and the wait can stretch for months. For a young family, a senior managing a chronic condition, or a newcomer still learning the system, that gap is not an abstraction. It is a real source of stress, and it pushes people toward walk-in clinics and emergency rooms for care that a family doctor could handle better.

## Why it hits Ward 2 harder

Our ward is home to many newcomer and multi-generational households. When a family arrives without an established doctor, the whole household feels it: the grandparent who needs regular monitoring, the parent booking a child's vaccinations, the adult child trying to coordinate it all across languages. The need is felt at the level of the family, not the individual.

## What a councillor can actually do

A city councillor does not run the health system. Deciding how doctors are funded and where they practise is a provincial responsibility. What a councillor can do is real, and it matters:

- **Advocate.** Press the province and local health partners to bring more primary care into Markham, and speak up when Ward 2 is underserved.
- **Inform.** Help residents understand and use the programs that already exist, in the language they speak at home, rather than leaving them to decode English-only government websites alone.
- **Connect.** Link residents and community organisations with the right services, and keep a door open when people do not know where to turn.

I will be honest about that line between what the city controls and what it does not. Pretending a councillor can hand out a family doctor would be a promise I could not keep.

## The work ahead

Over the coming weeks I will publish plain-language guides to finding primary care in our area, and I will keep pressing for more of it. If you have a story about your own search for a doctor, I want to hear it.
