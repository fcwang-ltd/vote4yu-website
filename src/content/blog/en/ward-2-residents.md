Markham Ward 2 is a place of families, seniors, newcomers, and long-time neighbours. Before any platform, it helps to name the problems people actually carry. This brief is about those shared challenges. It is not a list of promises, and it does not claim that a city councillor can deliver provincial or federal health benefits. A councillor advocates, informs, and connects residents to the right services.

## Six challenges residents talk about

### Preventive health that fits how people live

Many Ward 2 residents, including a large Chinese-Canadian community, find that everyday preventive care does not always match language needs, cultural comfort, or eldercare preferences. When care feels hard to reach or hard to trust, families delay check-ins and lean on emergency rooms instead of steady prevention.

### Family-doctor waits

Long waits to be accepted by a family doctor leave families, seniors, and newcomers anxious. Without a regular doctor, chronic conditions are harder to manage, and parents with young children struggle to keep up with routine care.

### Eldercare that respects culture and family

As more households care for aging parents, culturally adapted eldercare options remain scarce. Sandwich-generation adults often face career strain, financial pressure, and the emotional weight of choosing between inadequate institutional options and overwhelming home care.

### Housing and neighbourhood change

For many Markham homeowners, the house is the main store of family wealth. Softening markets and development pressure create a twin worry: protect property value and neighbourhood character while still allowing responsible growth.

### Everyday safety

Break-ins, auto theft, and scams that target seniors and newcomers feel personal, even when citywide statistics look abstract. Fear reduces mobility and trust, especially for people living alone or navigating English as a second language.

### A councillor who is present

Residents often feel a gap between council chambers and street-level life. Without consistent presence in community spaces and clear help navigating city services, small problems grow and civic trust erodes.

## Who calls Ward 2 home

Ward 2 includes Chinese-Canadian seniors who want independence and dignity in care; sandwich-generation families balancing elders and children; homeowners watching development and property value; younger renters and first-time buyers focused on affordability and transit; family health decision-makers; and long-time residents who care about taxes, infrastructure, and neighbourhood character.

These neighbours do not all want the same thing. They do share a desire for a councillor who listens in more than one language, shows up locally, and treats health as a through-line of municipal work.
